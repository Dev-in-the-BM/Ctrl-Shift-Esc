# The Kyocera Duras

The Kyocera Dura series are definitely from the best basic flip phones.

Their name says it all: They're extremely rugged, and are easily the most durable flip phones out there.

The interface is simple, utitliterian, making it feel like a true dumbphone. You'd never guess that it runs Android under the hood if you didn't know.

Basically, everything just works, it lasts long, and that's what people love about it.

## Which one?

There are multiple variants that all look very similiar, leaving some confused about what are the differences between them, and which to get.

The truth is that there are only a few differences between them, and its pretty simple.

Kyocera made different variants for each network, and each one only works on the network it was made for.

There are also some small software differences between the models.
The bootloaders are all locked and unlockable, making flashing and rooting dependant on model specific exploits.
The e4610 and e4810 has be rooted, while the others haven't.

Kyocera went with a naming scheme that I find a little confusing, so I just refer to them by their model numbers.

### The Verizon models

The e4610, e4810, e4811, and e4831 were made for Verizon, and only work on Verizon carriers.
That doesn't mean being stuck to Verizons expensive plans, they work perfectly with US Mobile and Red Pocket Mobiles cheap Verizon based plans.

The e4610 has terrific T9, and Verizons Message+, which many have considered to be the peak of dumbphone messaging.
On the other hand, it runs Android 7 and has an older processor, so it might not be the best pick if you plan on running third party smartphone apps.

The newer models come with a different T9 and messaging, which many report to be very lousy. However, you can just sideload SSpanaks excellent Traditional T9, and one of the [third party texting apps built for dumbphones](https://jtechforums.org/t/dpad-messaging/6790).

The e4810 and e4811 run Android 9, and the e4831 runs Android 10

### If you're on At&t

The e4830 is very similar to the e4810, but is made for At&t and its MVNOs.

Like the e4810, its default messaging and T9 isn't great, and I'd recommend installing third party replacements.

## Going cheap

The TCL Flip 2 was very popular as an ultra cheap option.

It's price has gone up, but you can still find it for around $60 on eBay.

Its downside is that it's pretty junky, it can take just a few falls to break the screen.
On the other hand, many who took care of it have had it working for 2 years.

Like the Kyocera, everything on it just works as it should, which is one of the reasons its so popular.

If you plan on sideloading apps, then things are a bit more complicated than the Kyoceras, but it's still possible.
Out of the box, the phone blocks all app intalls, even through ADB, and you have to flash a patched boot image to be able to install apps on it.

There are many guides online how to do this.

Rooting is relatively easy, and there's more documentation on this than for any other dumbphone.

# Sonims

## Sonim Xp3800

The Sonim XP3800 is another budget option, and can be found on eBay for around $50.

It's rugged, and can take a lot more than the the TCL.

Its main weak point is that its keypad eventually starts acting up, missing presses and double pressing.

Some of it's UX isn't as intuitive as other phones, with many options being hidden in context menus that you'd only see by pressing the menu key.
Most of the phone is pretty simple, though, and easy to pick up.

## Sonim Xp3900

The Sonim Xp3900, also known as the Xp3Plus, is a substantial upgrade over the Xp3800.

It's keypad is reliable, and the UX is as simple as any other flip phone.

It has double the storage and RAM, one of the fastest processors of all flip phones, and its processor is still competitive with the newest wave of modern dumbphones in 2026.

Its battery beats every other flip phone on this list, easily lasting 2-3 days, and is able to last more than a week on standby.

It has an 8 MP camera, which while significantly weaker than the cameras in smartphones, or even the newer generation of premium dumbphone, it's still better than any other traditional dumbphone.

Overall, the Xp3Plus works very smoothly, and is one of my favorite dumbphones out there.

Since it's based on Android 11, it runs into problems withs cursor apps, making it hard to impossible to use apps built for touchscreens.
The solution to that is to use the C9 cursor app with Shizuku.
Rooting it makes Shizuku a lot more reliable, and not need to be restarted manually on boot.

It's main drawback is that it's very bulky.

Other than that its pretty much the perfect flip phone.
