When I first heard about the Megalife F1, it sounded like a Cat S22 with Custom OS.

Then I looked up it's specs, and it was clear that although it has a lot in common with the Cat S22, it is *not* the same device.

The Cat S22 runs Android 11, has 2 GB RAM, and 16 GB storage.

The Megalige runs Android 13, 4 GB RAM, and 64 GB storage.

*Now* I wanted to test it out.

Eventually, I managed to get my hands on one.

I've used both the Sonim Xp3Plus and Kyocera DuraXe, which are both rugged and clunky as my main phone for a while, so although I've heard that the Cat S22 is large, I wasn't too concerned about the Megalifes size.

Then I got to hold it in my hands, and that's when I finally understood what people meant: It's a ***monster***.

But the truth is, that as bulky as it is, I quickly got used to it, and pretty soon I didn't even notice.

It has the regular Android 13 M3 UI, with its base apps being Google apps, so the phone doesn't feel extremely dumb or minimmalist, but it is restricted, and has a small screen, and for many that's a good compromise for a detox phone.

The phone uses Googles Apps for it's basic apps, so it comes with Google Messages, Contacts, Dialer, and GBoard.

RCS works in Google Messages.

The Megalife is a kosher phone, which means that it doesn't have a browser or  allow installing apps.

However, it does offer it's own app store, which offers it's own curated selection of utility apps, including email, authentication, ride share, travel, and medical apps.

There's also an option to get a limited version of WhatsApp.

It also offers Traditional T9, which I consider a major plus.
