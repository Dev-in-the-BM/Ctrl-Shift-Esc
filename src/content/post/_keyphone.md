When I first heard about Keyphone, I was pretty impressed.

Between the hardware and features clearly being designed by them from scratch, it's modularity, and its custom minimal custom skin, it sounded very cool.

I kept listening out for updates, waiting to hear more details and news about it.

Eventually, Keyphone was available for preorder and I decided to go for it.

I got mine in the beginning of this year.


Although it didn't become my daily driver, I give it a few test runs, using it as my main phone for a few days at a time, and every time I went back to it, two things hit me right away: How it feels holding it, and it's perfect UI design.

### Perfect form factor

Most of the phones I've used as my daily drivers were big and clunky, because the annoyance was worth the features or performance they had.
The Keyphone isn't just not clunky, it feels so *good* holding it.
Yeah, I know, they say it's "designed to live in your pocket, not in your hand", but you would've never guessed it.
It's a great size, feels solid, weight balanced, the form is justy right.

### Minimalist UI

The UI is another thing I feel they got right in a way no one else did.
Modern smartphones and apps are designed to be as appealing and immersive as possible, trying to suck you in every second of the day.
On the other extreme, there are dumbphones whose UI is functional but not necessarily very nice, and I personally prefer a more modern design.
Keyphone managed to achieve a beautiful balance, getting things right in the middle.
Modern, clean, and minimal.

### Innovative features and design

In a market of dumbphones with UX mostly inspired by either Android or old school dumbphones, Keyphone brings a breath of fresh air.
It's launcher is unique, with a layout that different, but easy to use, and optimized for a dumbphone.
They have their own custom notification and recent apps screens, which are simpler and a lot better suited to a small screen button phone, and a quick settings screen is supposed to be on the way as well.

The phone gets frequent OTA updates, bringing improvements, bug fixes, and new features.
Upcoming features include options for Android Auto, email, and hotspot.

This is another area I think they're doing a great job on, with the updates having a good balance of bugs fixes, adding essential missing functionality, and fancier features.

It also sounds like Keyphone is considering offering their own banking apps, productivity apps with Google sync, and RCS in their own messaging app, which would be exciting, innovative, and ambitious.

Also on the roadmap is allowing you to map the three buttons on the left side to do whatever you want, open an app, play a specific song, or toggle the flashlight.

It has an option for a physical QWERTY keyboard, and that was the feature I missed the most going back to my Wonder.
The buttons feel and click great.
I've heard some say that the QWERTY 
keyboard is too small to be usable, but  while I agree that it's small, I don't think it's *too* small, and find typing works great on it.

---

### What Keyphone *doesn't* have

The Keyphone is designed to be a simpler, locked down phone, which on the one hand means less distractions, but on the other hand means that it can't have features you might want.

Things Keyphone doesn't have:

- No sideloading apps
- No flashing custom ROMs
- No WhatsApp
- No RCS
- No music streaming

These are all things that many dumbphones don't have, and are part of choosing to live with a dumbphone.

---

### What I don't like about Keyphone


While overall the Keyphone is a great option, no phone is perfect, and the Keyphone has one big flaw: It's dpad is *very* small. 

The system updates automatically, without asking you, which many would find problematic.

Overall, it's a great phone, keeps getting better with each update, and can be a great option if you're looking for a quality, polished, dumbphone.
