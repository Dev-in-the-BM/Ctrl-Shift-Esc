Beginners guide to the dumbphone market

These days, the world is more digital than ever, and everything in life seems to be moving online.

Along with all this, smartphones are devouring peoples lives whole, and some are waking up to the fact that this has gone *way* too far.

Many are opting to switch to simpler devices to control their screen time and reclaim their lives, but the options can be overwhelming.

Button phones, screen phones, flip phones, you don't give a flip - you just want to get a phone that works for you and get on with your life.

While the dumbphone market is very diverse, it shouldn't have to confuse you.

You don't need to know about every phone to figure out which one is for you.

Most minimalist phones fall into a few categories, each a different approach to how its dumbed down and what features they offer.

Once you know what types there are, you can decide which approach is for you, narrowing down your options to the handful that are in that category.

We're going to go through each one, outlining how each one does dumb, and which features they do and don't have.
I'm not going to give detailed comparisons of the phones in each category, as I'm just trying to give you a picture of what the dumbphone landscape looks like, so you can know what *type* of phone to look for.

I hope to write separate articles focusing on the best options in each category.

### 1: The Potato

This category includes many of AGM and HMD's phones.

These phone are both very basic and cheap, and often look like dumbphones from 10 years ago.

They usually run S30+ or ThreadX.

While many who just want talk and text are attracted to them, as they seem to be a great basic option, I recommend strongly against buying them.

Their firmware is half baked, making even basic functionality difficult to use.

Predictive T9 might be half functional or non existant, and MMS group messaging nonexistant.

Text messaging are shown in inbox and outbox folders like email, instead of the conversation view we're used to, and often only 500-2,000 messages can be saved.

These phones one strong point is terrific battery life, but I don't think that makes it worth it.

### 2: KaiOs phones

KaiOs was designed to be a modern OS for flip phones, offering modern apps and features on modest hardware.

Unfortunately, in real life KaiOs phones are very laggy, making them very difficult to use.
While more recent version of KaiOs have addressed this, only the latest version, KaiOs 4.0, has fixed this, by requiring hardware that Android can run on. 

They have reliability issues, with the phones sometimes randomly glitching out.

They also come with uninstallable bloatware, including a browser and the KaiOs app store.

In short, KaiOs is good in theory, terrible in real life, and I say give these phones a hard pass.

### 3: Traditional flip phone on the outside, Android under the hood

Many, when they hear "Android", imagine a touchscreen device with an animated, slick UI.

These phones are *not* that.

They look and feel like true dumbphones.

Most don't have any animations, and have their own custom UI designed for the phones small screen and physical controls.

They *do* inherit Androids reliability, stability, and flexibility.

While most of these phones can install apps, they have no app store, and there are many free tools you can use to lock the phone down and make installing apps impossible.
These same tools also allow disabling many features that these phones ship with, including camera, media playback, and web browsing.

Overall, this is my favorite category of dumbphone, and whether you're looking for something simple and reliable or something that can run apps but still feel dumb, these are the phones I usually recommend.

### Modern kosher phone

Just to be clear, when I say *modern kosher phone*, I'm not talking about all phone that count as kosher phones.

I'm referring to a newer generation of kosher phones that has emerged in recent years, that are in many ways similar to the previous category.

While they both use Android and usually flip, the difference between them is that these kosher phones are more premium and restricted.

These phones usually have: 
- Better cameras
- A touchscreen
- Navigation
- Voice typing
- Android Auto
- Software updates

At the same time, they usuallly remove or block:
- App installation
- Browsing
- Video playback
- Custom ROMs
- Hotspot
- Calling and texting AI chatbots

These phones aren't all the same, though, some offer their own app store with a curated selection of utilities, and some allow hotspot or WhatsApp.

They usually put more work into UI and UX, delivering a product that's both easier to use while keeping things simpler and cleaner.

### Keypad phones and small screen phones

These phones are technically full fledged Android smartphones, and you can do anything on them that you can do on a smartphone.

Practically, though, their small screens help curb usage, allowing to use your phone more mindfully.

Some of these, like the Jelly Star, are just smaller versions of the glass slab you're used to, while others, like the Sidephone and Qins, have keypads, giving them more of a dumbphone feel.

For many these can be a good compromise between minimalism and functionality.

### The E-ink Monotone

Another interesting type of phone are e-ink Androids.

These include the Mudita Kompakt, BigMe Hibreak, and the original Minimal Phone.

These run full Android, and can do everything a standard can do, albeit in black  white, at the speed of e-ink.

Many e-ink screens aren't as fast as traditonal phone screens, which adds friction, and encourages more mindful use.
Removing all color from the phone also removes a lot of the appeal of consuming.

These features make for a phone that can do what you need, while being less tempting to oversuse.

### The Straitjacketed Smartphone

These phones are standard smartphones, that have been modified to restrict them to their own curated selection of utility apps.

This usually includes tools like navigation apps, messaging platforms, email, music streaming, sometimes limited browsers.

These phones obviously won't feel like a dumbphone at all.
They have the ease of use and slick UI of smartphones, which all encourage spending more time on even utility apps.

On the one hand, the biggest time wasters are blocked.

Some great options include the Gabb phone and Sleke phone.

### The Innovators

The last and most interesting category are phones that just do things different, using creative methods to give a simpler experience.

There's the Light Phone, which keeps the UI very simple.
 No animations, minimal graphics, and everything black and white.

There's the upcoming Basic Phone, which will have a black & white e-ink screen, QWERTY keyboard, a custom Linux based OS, and it's own apps and app store.

There's the Dumbphone 2, which took the TCL Flip 2, and loaded it with features and tools like music streaming, WhatsApp and iMessage, and
