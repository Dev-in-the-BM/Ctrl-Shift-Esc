In 2026, most of the world communicates using platforms like WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, and Google RCS to communicate.

Being free, very well featured, and most being accessible to almost everyone, these platforms are used in every area of life, from workplace discussions, group chats with families, and direct messaging and vocie calls.

The works for everyone besides for minimalist and kosher phone users.

Even most modern dumbphones don't support these platforms, either because they require the official apps, which can have distracting features and design, or because the phone is too dumb to be able to run the app.

People using these phones are usually forced to rely on SMS & MMS text messaging, which is too limited for many use cases, including most group chats.

### Enter Matrix

Matrix is an open source, decentralized messaging protocol.

That means that it's basically the messaging equivalent to email.

With email, you can open gmail, and email someone who uses Outlook, iCloud, Proton, or any other email service.

You can access your emails from any app that supports standard email protocols, like Thunderbird or K9, even though it has no affiliation with the email service you use. 

Anyone who knows what they're doing is free to setup their own email service, and it will work perfectly with every other email service out there.

Matrix works the same way.

No one owns Matrix.

You can use whichever Matrix server you want, and message someone using any other Matrix server.

You can use any Matrix app you want.

Anyone can host their own Matrix server.

### What does this have to with messaging on dumbphones?

This is the thing: Since Matrix is open to any app designed for Matrix, and any developer can build their own Matrix app, it's possible to build a Matrix client that's as minimalist as you want, including or leaving out whichever features you want.

You can have one that blocks media attachments, or groups over a certain size, or any other features of Matrix you don't like.

You can have Matrix clients that are dpad compatible or small screen friendly.

There are already phones like the Sunbeam that have this concept for email.

There's no reason not to have it for messaging.
