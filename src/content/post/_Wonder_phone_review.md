### Not just another overpriced flip phone

When I first heard about the Wonder phone, I was a bit skeptical.

Another $300+ locked down touchscreen flip phone? Who needed more of the same?

Wonder tried marketing themselves as a successor to the LG Exalt, but I had a hard time believing that such a small company could make something that polished.

A while later, I got my hands on a Wonder phone, and I completely changed my mind.

I ended up buying the Wonder and using it as my daily driver for six months, and really liked it. 

What's so great about it?

### Sleek UI, modern features

Its core apps are built in-house, from scratch .
Its UI is beautiful, polished, simple, and d-pad-native.
Everything feels cohesive, with a consistent experience across the phone. 
It has custom d-pad-native quick settings and notifications screens, which can be accessed by swiping the screen or through keyboard shortcuts.

It gets frequent system updates, fixing bugs and bringing new features.

Its voice typing is the best I've ever used on a simpler phone, and is powered by a state-of-the-art ASR model.

In addition to Wonder's suite of utility tools, it offers Waze, Android Auto, GBoard, ColorNote, and Musicolet, offering excellent navigation, and what I consider the best offline music player out there.
Google Maps is also available, but only on Android Auto. 

Its other features include weather and a dedicated feedback app.

Its battery life is excellent, easily lasting a day or two, depending on usage, and could probably last at least a week if you're not using it too much.

It also has a parental control feature that allows you to disable many of the phone's features, including the touchscreen, media playback, MMS, SMS, and any of its apps.
It's very secure, even persisting through full device resets.

### What the Wonder doesn't have

As a locked down kosher phone, there's no:
- Sideloading apps
- Flashing ROMs.
- RCS
- WhatsApp
- Music streaming
- Messaging AI services
- Playing videos that weren't taken on the phone
- Hotspot

### What I don't like about the Wonder

Its keypad is a bit too big, and the tactile feel isn't great, making typing using the keypad annoying.

I also think that the UI, while clean and minimal, is a bit too slick and animated, with too much eye candy, but that's just my opinion.

Its camera is decent, but I would expect better for the price.

The newer Wonder Pro reportedly has an improved camera, but I think that for the price the Wonder sells for, even the base model should have a better camera.

Its build quality isn't great, although they do offer free repairs for the first year after purchase, and the leather case does a great job protecting it.

Another frustrating point is that they seem to be prioritizing flashy new features over ux and bug fixes.
With a new phone made by a small company with minimal resources, some missing features, bugs, and UX quirks are to be expected.
I'm fine with that, as long as those issues are short-term, and the company prioritizes ironing them out.
While Wonder has significantly improved the phone's software, it still has things that need to be fixed, and over the past half a year they seem to have been prioritizing extras over essentials.
This is what ended up pushing me to another phone.

### Takeaway

The Wonder has many things going for it, with UX being one of its strong points.

I think that it can be an excellent option for those looking for a phone that offers smartphone UI polish and ease of use, in a simpler and more intentional package.
