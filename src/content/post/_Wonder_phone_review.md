### Not just another overpriced flip phone

When I first heard about the Wonder phone, I was a bit skeptical.

Another $300+ locked down touchscreen flip phone? Who needed more of the same?

They tried marketing themselves as a successor to the LG Exalt, but I had a hard time believing that {such a small company would be able to pull off such a good job}.

A while later, I got my hands on a Wonder phone, and I completely changed my mind.

I ended up using the Wonder as my daily driver for a half a year, and really liked it. 

What's so great about it?

### Sleek UI, modern features

It's core apps are built by their team from scratch.
It's UI is beautiful, polished, simple, and dpad native.
Everything feels cohesive, working the same across the phone. 
It has a custom dpad-native quick settings and notifications screens, which can be accessed by swiping the the screen or through keyboard shortcuts.

It gets frequent system updates, fixing bugs and bringing new features.

It has voice typing built in, and it's the best voice typing I've ever used on a simpler phone, powered by a state of the art ASR model.

In addition to Wonder's suite of utility tools, it also offers Waze, Android Auto, GBoard, ColorNote and Musicolet, {bringing} great navigation and the best offline music player out there.
Google Maps is also available, on Android Auto only. 

It's other features include weather and a dedicated feedback app.

It's camera is pretty good, but although it's autofocus has improved, it could still be better. 

It's battery life is excellent, easily lasting 1-2 days or more depending on usage, and could probably last at least a week if you're not using it too much.

It also has a parental control feature that allows you to disable many of the phones features, including the touchscreen, media payback, MMS, SMS, and any of it's apps.
It's very secure, even persisting through full device resets.

### What the Wonder doesn't have

As a locked down kosher phone, there's no:
- Sideloading apps
- Flashing ROMs.
- RCS
- WhatsApp
- Music streaming
- Messaging AI services
- Playing videos that weren't taken on the phone
- Hotspot

### What I don't like about the Wonder

Its keypad is a bit too big, and the tactile isn't great, making typing using the keypad annoying.

I also think that the UI, while clean and minimal, is a bit too slick and animated, with too much eye candy, but that's just my opinion.

It's camera isn't great, and I would expect better for the price.

The newer Wonder Pro reportedly has an improved camera, but I think that for the price the Wonder sells for, even the base model should have a better camera.

It's build quality isn't great, although they do offer free repairs for the first year after purchase, and the leather case does a great job protecting it.

Another point is that they seem to be prioritizing shinier festures over ux and bug fixes.
With a new phone made by a small company with minimal resources, some missing features, bugs, and UX {something} are to be expected, and I'm fine with that, as long as it's short term, and they prioritize ironing those out.
While Wonder has significantly improved the phones software, it still has things that need to be fixed, and in the past half a year they seem to have been prioritizing extras over essentials.
This is what ended up pushing me to another phone.

### Takeaway

I've used the Wonder for a while, and found it to have many plusses, with UX being one of its strong points.

I think that it can be an excellent option for those looking for a phone that offers smartphone UI polish and ease of use, in a simpler and more intentional package.
